For regulated work

An answer for the day someone asks why.

In lending, insurance and healthcare, an AI decision is not finished when it is made. It is finished when it can be explained to an applicant, an examiner or a court, months later. Vertep records the process as it happens, so the explanation is a replay and not a reconstruction.

Three places it fits

The pattern is the same: the model takes the routine cases, the chart refuses what policy forbids, and a person owns the consequential ones.

The questions an examiner asks

And where each answer lives. None of these needs a separate logging system, because the record is what ran.

QuestionWhere the answer is
What was the process in when it decided?The state configuration as of the decision's journal entry (state_at).
What was it allowed to do?The enabled events the chart offered at that decide state. Anything else was not on the table.
Who or what chose, and what did they say?The decision entry: the chosen event, the decider's identity and its rationale, kept as the decider's claim.
Under which version of the rules?The chart's version is the hash of its canonical form, recorded with the entries.
Was a person involved, and when?The chart names which states are decided by people. A person's decision is a journal entry like any other.
Can you reproduce it?Replay the journal with no decider bound: same states, no model calls.
What would the new rules have done?Fork from the journal under another chart version and compare, without touching the original record.

What it contributes to the rules you answer to

A record is evidence. It is not compliance. This table says which part of each requirement the record helps with, and which stays yours.

RegimeWhat it asks, in shortWhat Vertep contributes, and what it does not
EU AI Act, Articles 12, 13 and 14Automatic recording of events, transparency to deployers, and human oversight, for high-risk systems such as credit scoring and life or health insurance risk assessment.The journal is an automatic, ordered event record; human-decided states make oversight structural. Classification, risk management and conformity assessment stay with you.
GDPR, Articles 15(1)(h) and 22Meaningful information about the logic of automated decisions, and limits on decisions based solely on automated processing.The chart and journal show the logic and the human steps. Whether a given decision needs a human in the loop is a legal call; the chart lets you build one in.
GDPR, Article 17Erasure of personal data.An append-only journal is in tension with this. An agent journal wrapped in a SealingJournal stores the fields a SealPolicy marks sensitive as ciphertext under a per-subject key; erase destroys the key and appends an erasure entry, so the chain and signatures still verify, and expire erases subjects past their retention period. The only key store shipped keeps keys in memory, so a durable store that really destroys keys is yours. The chart's types mark what to seal (@sensitive, @subject, @retain), a replay after an erasure follows the outcome each entry recorded instead of re-evaluating a guard that reads an erased value, and erasure retracts the rule-derived facts that rest on the subject.
US ECOA and Regulation BSpecific reasons when credit is denied.The journal records the decline and its rationale, and the chart can require a reason on the event. Choosing lawful reason codes is yours.
SR 11-7 model risk managementInventory, controls and ongoing monitoring of models used in decisions.A model's role is bounded and logged at named decide states, which gives a clear inventory of where models act. Validation of the model itself stays with you.
FDA 21 CFR Part 11Secure, time-stamped audit trails for electronic records in regulated life-science work.Journals are append-only and time-stamped. Durable objects keep signed, hash-chained journals, and an agent journal in a store can opt in to the same chain. Validation, access control and signatures are your system's work.

This is a technical summary, not legal advice, and Vertep is a tool, not a certification. Obligations depend on your jurisdiction, product and risk classification; check them with counsel.

Said plainly: what it does not do

  • It does not open the model. A model's stated rationale is recorded as its claim, labelled as such. It is not a faithful account of the weights, and Vertep does not pretend otherwise. What is verifiable is the process around the model.
  • It does not make a policy fair. A chart can encode a biased threshold exactly. It makes the threshold visible, versioned and testable, which is where fairness review has to start.
  • It erases by destroying keys, not entries. A sealed field becomes unreadable; the entry, its place in the order and every field not marked sensitive stay. See the Article 17 row above for what stays with you.
  • It is 0.1.x software. It has not run a lender's production load. Use it first where a failure is cheap, and where its record is worth more than its maturity.
  • A plain journal is not signed, but it can be. A durable object's journal is hash-chained and host-signed; an agent journal in a store can opt in to the same chain and signature (ChainedAgentSealer), and the object's own verifier accepts it. An exportable explanation bundle exists and vt verify-bundle checks it offline, naming an altered entry or a missing chart; the bundle's manifest is not itself signed, so someone who rewrites the entries and the manifest together makes a bundle that verifies, and the signature on a chained journal is what a bundle should be checked against.
  • It is not a compliance product. It gives you an honest record and structural limits. The programme around them is yours.

Start with one decision

Pick one consequential decision a model currently makes or drafts. Write its legal moves as a chart. Bind the model at the one state where it has to choose.
