The book

The Goat Does Not Choose the Play

Your agent did something, and a person with the right to ask wants to know why. “The model said so” is not an answer. This book teaches one architecture and then builds it, in a domain where the question is not optional: consumer credit decisions.

The picture

The liveliest figure on the stage does not choose the play.

In the Ukrainian vertep, the travelling nativity puppet theatre, the goat is the liveliest figure on the stage. She dances, she is struck down, she comes back to life, and the crowd loves her. But she does not choose the play. That is the right picture of a language model inside a program: clever, lively, and not to be trusted with the whole plot.

The book's answer is a statechart that offers the moves, a decider (a language model, a person or a rule set) that may only pick among them, and a journal in which every input is recorded. The model is not deterministic; the run is, because the model's answer is recorded as an input. So the run replays exactly, and the record explains itself.

One running case

Kalyna Credit, a fictional lender, decides consumer loans with a model’s help. You read its legal obligations as an engineer, read its record as an auditor would, and operate it in production.

The book is written against Vertep 0.1.7, the open-source Rust system on this site. But it is about the architecture: every concept is explained so that a reader on another stack can carry it away. Where it describes what a regulation asks of a system, it gives an engineer’s reading and tells you to check it with counsel, and the last chapter lists what the architecture does not answer and when you should not use it.

Who it is for

  • Engineers and architects building agents, LLM-driven or not, that must be able to say afterwards what happened and why.
  • Technical leads who will be asked by a compliance officer to explain the design.
  • Compliance, risk and audit people who have been handed a design and a suspicion.

You should be able to read code (Python or TypeScript is fine), have called an LLM API, and know what a state machine is in the loose sense. You do not need to know Harel statecharts, SCXML, actors, Datalog or Prolog, or session types: chapters 3 to 6 teach them.
